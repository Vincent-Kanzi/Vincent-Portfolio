# Agile Team Charter

## Evidence
> ![Team Charter](assets/team-charter.png)


This is the team charter my internship team and I created for WHIRLME SYSTEM
at CPUT. It defined our shared purpose, values, working agreements and
operational boundaries while we delivered responsive project that will help house parents with their residences incidents.

### What we agreed on
| Element | Our agreement |
|---|---|
| Shared purpose | Deliver a working increment of WHIRLME every sprint |
| Core values | Transparency, respect, accountability, continuous improvement |
| Working agreements | Daily stand-up at 08H30; respond on work duties within 9 hours |
| Definition of Done | Code reviewed, tested, documented, merged to main |
| Roles | Scrum Master: Celeste; Product Owner: Palesa; Developers: Vasco, Rethabile and I |

## Reflection
Before this internship I thought a team charter was something a lecturer imposes
on you I had made one for coursework and barely used it. Writing one *with* a
professional team changed that. In my first week I noticed friction people
joining stand ups late, tasks with no clear owner and I recognised it as a
**charter gap**, not a personality problem, because I had seen the same pattern
in my earlier projects. The clause that mattered most was our Definition of Done,
because it stopped the repeated "is this finished?" arguments I remembered from
QuizApp and ETL.

I also learned a charter is only alive if you use it. Ours was reviewed at the
start of every retrospective, and we amended it once to add a  blocked work 
escalation rule after a dependency stalled. That is evidence of my growth 
from treating documents as paperwork to treating them as instruments that
change how a team behaves.
